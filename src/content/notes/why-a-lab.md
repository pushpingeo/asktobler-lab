GIS work has always come in three parts you get separately. The software, which you buy and learn. The data, which you find, clean and argue about. And the know-how, which mostly lives in people's heads.

AI is starting to pull those together. You can ask for a piece of GIS work in plain English and get something back that runs. Not always right. Often enough that I've stopped thinking of AI as a feature on the side of GIS, and started thinking of it as the way in.

I don't think that makes GIS analysts redundant. I think it makes a good one faster, and more thorough. But that's only an opinion, and the honest thing is to test it.

## What goes in here

Small things, each trying to show one thing:

- **Projects** you can try. Most run in your browser on open data.
- **Notes** on what I tried and what happened. Including the bits that didn't work. My first go at an AI site-screening skill made the model *worse*, for example. That one's coming soon, with the numbers.

Some of it will end up in Tobler, a site-screening tool I'm building. Most of it won't, and that's fine. A lab is allowed to have dead ends.

## The name

Waldo Tobler's first law of geography says that everything is related to everything else, but near things are more related than distant things.

It's the nearest thing my field has to a motto. It's also most of site screening in a sentence. And it turns out maps and AI are very near things.

Everything here is built in my own time, on open data, and the views are my own. If something is useful, or wrong, I'd like to hear about it.
