🌐 **The Future Ecosystem is Here - Where's the Spatial Analysis?** 🌍

The [**Future Geospatial Information Ecosystem**](https://ggim.un.org/meetings/GGIM-committee/15th-session/documents/2025-07_UN-GGIM_FutureGeospatialEcosystem.pdf) position paper, shared by the [UNGGIM](https://ggim.un.org/) for their 15th session this week, marks a powerful shift in thinking and one that better reflects the realities of the 21st-century.

It’s built on the solid foundations of the [**Integrated Geospatial Information Framework**](https://ggim.un.org/UN-IGIF/) **(IGIF)** and broader UN-led initiatives like the **Sustainable Development Goals**.  It’s worth exploring the [supporting documents](https://ggim.un.org/meetings/GGIM-committee/15th-Session/documents/E_C20_2025_07_Add.1.pdf) that reveal the depth of collaboration and insight behind this work.

What’s refreshing is the candid recognition of the limitations of **Spatial Data Infrastructures (SDIs)**. For years, as the **API** economy surged ahead, I often felt many GIS professionals were locked in a metadata dungeon, dutifully editing records and championing openness while innovation raced ahead elsewhere. Now, we’re talking about a **‘System of Systems’** and a **‘Geoverse’** - a welcome evolution.

In the spirit of continuing the dialogue, while the paper powerfully reimagines infrastructure, governance, and interoperability, I wondered what of the role for **Spatial Analysis**?

It’s spatial analysis which ties our data to decisions and outcomes. Reading the position paper it feels more implied than explicit, is spatial analysis a downstream activity and not of relevance to geo-information management?

If we want to greater influence frameworks like the SDG’s, which have been heavily influenced by economics and statistics, are we considering how techniques like **GIS-MCDA**, **GeoDesign** & **Agent Based Modelling** can influence and integrate into broader frameworks?
