Reading around the LGND press release that I shared yesterday, led me to this recently published, sobering video from Geoffrey Hinton.  The 'God father of AI' and a recent Nobel Prize winner, he serves up a masterclass in how to make a difficult topic accessible, it's very much worth watching 🎥

A man who carries the inevitable scars of a long and successful career (alongside the Nobel Prize 🏅), it appears Hinton has spent years facing challenge from linguists on the very structure of language.

As a geographer, it made me pause: what does this mean for our own 'laws of geography'—and their relevance in a world to be shaped by neural networks?

Where better to start than **Tobler’s First Law of Geography**:

> “Everything is related to everything else, but near things are more related than distant things.” — Waldo Tobler

This principle has shaped many aspects of spatial analysis; but just as Hinton disrupted decades of linguistic theory by showing that meaning could emerge from statistical relationships between words, not formal rules, how important will Tobler's laws be in a new world of transformers and geographic encodings? Curious if anyone can point me to further research on this, or an informed opinion, learning as I go.
